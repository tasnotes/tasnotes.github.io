Even though I've gone through The Book of Proofs which details all the ways in which to understand what the hell goes on in a proof I still find myself reading a theory, "understanding" its proof, then wasting hours or even days on a couple problems. And so I stumbled across a [Tedx video]( https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cbiyPOn-__M) on my youtube algorithm today and it struck me as oddly familiar. You know that phenomenon of a recently learned word seemingly appearing everywhere? Yeah, apparently it's called the frequency illusion and the most recent word for me was itinerary. Anyway, in summary the speaker states that the brain is a vast archive of knowledge with an intrinsic ability to produce new information from old. While it seems obvious enough, it's not something people are consciously aware of and its true capabilities for learning. That familiarity was the learning method the speaker presents that resembles the socratic method except instead of teacher-student dialogue, it's a series of self probing questions and explanations in an effort to uncover logical inconsistencies. Thus I've once again become aware of the socratic method and how truly awful my study methods are, evident by my academic records. But! I have ADHD so I take no blame. Partial blame.
